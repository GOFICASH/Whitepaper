# The problem

Crypto is easy to hold and hard to spend. Around 741 million people own crypto, most of them in Asia, Africa and Latin America. Turning a wallet balance into something useful still runs through a bank, an exchange and an identity check.

Three everyday needs show the gap:

* **Travel data.** Travellers land, look for a SIM shop and pay roaming prices. A travel eSIM costs about $16 a trip against $41 for roaming, but the major eSIM apps accept cards, not wallets.
* **Spending online.** Most crypto cards are offered only in the US, Europe and a handful of other markets. Wise, Revolut and MetaMask Card are unavailable across much of Asia and Africa.
* **Verification.** Account sign-ups and two-factor codes require a phone number, which usually means a local SIM and an ID.

The infrastructure to close this gap now exists. Visa supports more than 160 stablecoin-linked card programs, annual crypto card spending has passed $18 billion, and travel eSIM revenue grew 85% to $1.8 billion in 2025. What is missing is a consumer product built around the wallet.
